We write for someone who is interested but not yet convinced. That reader is generous with attention and impatient with fog.

## Sentences

Prefer one idea per sentence. If a sentence has three commas and a semicolon, it is probably two sentences wearing a coat.

## Claims

Make the claim specific enough to disagree with. "This is better" is not a claim. "This removes a confirmation step without hiding the consequence" is.

## Examples

Use real examples from the work. If the example needs a fictional company, keep the details believable and the lesson honest.

## Formatting

- Use sentence case for headings.
- Use `code` for file names, commands, and field names.
- Use a table when comparing three or more things.
- Use a callout for a warning or a rule of thumb, not for emphasis.

<Callout variant="warning">
  Do not use a callout for every paragraph. If everything is highlighted, the
  highlight stops meaning anything.
</Callout>

## Final check

Read the piece aloud. The places where you stumble are usually the places where the thinking is still unfinished.
