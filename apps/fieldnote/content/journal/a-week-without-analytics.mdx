The analytics dashboard had become a morning habit. Open the laptop, check the numbers, decide how the day was going. For one week we replaced it with a note in the shared journal: *what did we learn from a person this week?*

## What we expected

We expected to feel uncertain. We did, a little. We also expected the numbers to be the only reliable signal, which turned out to be the least interesting part of the experiment.

## What actually happened

Three things changed almost immediately.

1. We asked more direct questions. Without a chart to interpret, we wrote to the people using the site.
2. We stopped optimising the first five minutes. The dashboard rewarded arrival; our readers were telling us about return visits.
3. We shipped one small fix that no metric would have surfaced, because it came from a sentence in a reply.

<Callout variant="tip">
  This is not an argument against measurement. It is an argument for keeping
  measurement in proportion.
</Callout>

## What we kept

When the week ended, we did not delete the dashboard. We moved it. It is now a weekly review rather than a daily reflex, and the journal question stayed.

The useful metric was never on the dashboard. It was whether we could describe, in plain language, what someone had trouble with. If we could, we knew what to do next.
