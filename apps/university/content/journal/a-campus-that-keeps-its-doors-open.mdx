The best part of a university is not usually scheduled. It is the hour after a seminar when two students keep arguing about the reading, or the ordinary Tuesday evening when the common room fills because the library is still open.

We have tried to design for that hour.

## Time is part of the built environment

A campus can look generous and still be closed in practice. If the last bus leaves before the library closes, the building is not really open. If the common room has no kettle, it is not really a common room. If the calendar is hard to read, people stop checking it.

So we treat opening hours, transport, and the calendar as one system rather than three administrative details.

## Small choices, repeated

- Keep the library open until midnight during the final two weeks of term.
- Publish the calendar as a calendar, not a PDF or a list of announcements.
- Leave the common room unlocked after hours, with a clear note about who is responsible for locking up.
- Make every event state who it is for, even when the answer is "everyone".

<Callout variant="note">
  A calendar is a promise about where people can be. It should be as easy to read
  as the timetable on the back of a door.
</Callout>

## What we are still learning

The open-door approach works best when the campus is small. It asks staff to trust students with space, and it asks students to leave the room as they found it. That trust is not a policy. It is a habit, and habits need maintenance.

This term we are trying a simple test: for every event we publish, ask whether a first-year student could tell from the listing alone whether they belong there. When the answer is no, the listing gets another draft.
